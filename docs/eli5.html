What is new in this paper?

A plain-words note for One World or Two: Where Ax1Gen Reads Φ, and Gödel’s Th3 in S4.

Imagine a rulebook for a game about “God,” written in a form a computer can check.

Someone copied one rule from an old notebook into that rulebook. The copy looks fine. The paper asks: if you play by that copied rule exactly as written, what happens?

The rulebook on the computer is Scott’s repaired copy, not Gödel’s own handwritten page. Gödel’s page left out a piece, and Scott put that piece back. Our new bit is still about where one copied rule looks when you read it exactly as written.

What we found is this. With that copied rule, every place in the game can only “see” itself. Nothing connects to anything else. Then the big claim people cared about (“if God is possible, God is necessary”) becomes true for a boring reason: in a world that only sees itself, whatever is true is already “necessary.”

Other people had already shown that boring ending, but they had to add an extra assumption (“you can always look both ways”). We show the same ending without that extra assumption. That is the new bit.

We also show that if you fix the copied rule in either of two careful ways, so it matches what Gödel actually meant better, then the boring ending goes away. On a simple two-step chain of worlds, the big claim can fail. So the open question is not “does the claim hold in a weaker logic?” under the literal copy. Under the literal copy there is almost nothing left to ask. The live question sits with the repaired rules.

The paper does not claim to be the first ever to notice collapse in this family of proofs. It claims one sharper fact: the collapse follows from the literal copy alone, with no “look both ways” assumption.
