Plain words

A plain-words note for One World or Two: Where AFP Figure 7 Reads Φ.

Imagine a rulebook for a game about “God,” written in a form a computer can check.

Someone copied one rule from an old notebook into that rulebook. The copy looks fine. The paper asks: if you play by that copied rule exactly as written, what happens?

The rulebook on the computer is Scott’s repaired copy, not Gödel’s own handwritten page. Gödel’s page left out a piece, and Scott put that piece back. The note is about where that copied rule looks when you read it exactly as written.

What we found is this. With that copied rule, every place in the game can only “see” itself. Nothing connects to anything else. Then the big claim people cared about (“if God is possible, God is necessary”) becomes true for a boring reason: in a world that only sees itself, whatever is true is already “necessary.” Superseded 2 Oct 2026: see Benzmüller arXiv:2609.36279 v2 §4, Theorem 4(a) (Th3, in K, with no frame condition and no nominal), Theorem 4(b) (collapse) and Theorem 4(c) (OneWorld, by a nominal). Our proof also used a degenerate, empty case of the copied rule; see the paper, §1 and §4.

Other people had already shown that boring ending by assuming you can always look both ways. On this copied rule the axioms themselves produce that both-ways link, and then each place can see only itself. The claim holds for that dull reason. Superseded 2 Oct 2026: see Benzmüller arXiv:2609.36279 v2 §4, Theorem 4(c) (OneWorld) and Theorem 4(a)–(b) The live question sits with the repaired rules.

Two repairs make both halves of the copied rule look at the roster in the same place. On those repairs the boring ending goes away. On a simple two-step chain of worlds, the big claim can fail. (For the boxed repair, Benzmüller reports the same thing: arXiv:2609.36279 v2 §6.3, “Ax1GenBox”.) On the literal copy the claim holds only because each place already sees only itself, including the one-place model. Superseded 2 Oct 2026: see Benzmüller arXiv:2609.36279 v2 §4, Theorem 4(a) The live question sits with the repaired rules.

The paper does not claim to be the first to notice collapse in this family of proofs. On the literal copy, the claim holds only because each place already sees only itself. Superseded 2 Oct 2026: see Benzmüller arXiv:2609.36279 v2 §4, Theorem 4(a)–(c) The repairs are where that ending can fail.

What is still ours (2 Oct 2026). For the repair that keeps the roster the same everywhere, the key lemma (“being God-like is a good property”) still follows. Benzmüller’s files list that as open. Our lemma_L_rigid builds on the theory GoedelVariantHOML2inS4 in Benzmüller and Scott’s AFP entry Notes_On_Goedels_Ontological_Argument, and its snapshot-and-agreement step uses the same move as Th4_finite in arXiv:2608.07578; our commit b23cc28 cited arXiv:2608.07578, for a different result, 31 minutes before 27524f7, and our records do not show whether Th4_finite was read. It proves L for the rigid-Φ variant he later lists as Candidate B (Ax1GenR), in commit 27524f7, merged 24 Sep 2026 (20:51 UTC), four days before arXiv:2609.36279 v1 (28 Sep). We checked arXiv:2609.26806 v1, the Monatshefte paper, the AFP entries, and his public repositories as of that date and found no statement of Candidate B or of L as open under it. We also checked a new version of the rule that combines that repair with his “at least two conjuncts” rule. The details and their limits are in §6 of the paper.
