\begin{abstract}
Benzm\"uller and Scott leave open whether Theorem Th3 of their actualist
Figure~7---possible existence of a God-like being implies necessary
existence, with \(P(G)\) derived from Ax1Gen, the AFP abbreviation of the
footnote's generalization---is provable in S4, and they leave two related
questions open \cite{benzmuellerScott2025}.
Benzm\"uller has since settled all three \cite{benzmuellerPWN2026}.
Under Ax1Gen, Th3 holds already in K, by a hybrid-free proof that turns on
the empty conjunction.
Under his emendation Ax1GenTwo, which requires at least two different
conjuncts, the questions are settled again, hybrid-free under stated side
conditions, and for Figure~8 only after a further emendation.
The answer this note gave first is superseded by that paper, and it is weaker
in two ways.
The Ax1Gen instance it checked, in session
\lean{Ax1Gen\_EmptyAtHome\_Check} on Isabelle2025-2, is empty at the world
of evaluation, so it is one of the degenerate instances that Ax1GenTwo
excludes.
Its witnesses are a nominal and a world-indexed collection, and Benzm\"uller's
Lean detector flags both as \lean{NOMINAL RIGID}.
The symmetry, identity, and collapse theorems below are therefore theorems
of the shallow embedding with full comprehension, not of the modal object
language.
What remains of this note is a comparison of where Ax1Gen reads \(\Phi\).
Two reconstructions make both halves of Ax1Gen read \(\Phi\) at the same
world: positivity inside the box, or a world-invariant \(\Phi\).
Both yield \(P(G)\) and fail on a two-world S4 chain.
For the world-invariant reading, Benzm\"uller's ancillary session~B lists the
derivability of \(P(G)\) as open.
Our \lean{lemma\_L\_rigid} builds on Benzm\"uller's AFP entry \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2inS4}~\cite{afpNotes2025}, and its snapshot-and-agreement step uses the same move as \lean{Th4\_finite} in arXiv:2608.07578~\cite{benzmueller2026}; our commit \lean{b23cc28} cited arXiv:2608.07578, for a different result, 31~minutes before \lean{27524f7}, and our records do not show whether \lean{Th4\_finite} was read.
It proves L for the rigid-\(\Phi\) variant he later lists as Candidate~B (Ax1GenR), in commit \lean{27524f7}, merged 24~September 2026 (20:51~UTC), four days before arXiv:2609.36279 v1 (28~September)~\cite{benzmuellerPWN2026}.
We checked arXiv:2609.26806 v1~\cite{benzmuellerLean2026}, the Monatshefte paper~\cite{benzmuellerScott2025}, the AFP entries, and his public repositories as of that date and found no statement of Candidate~B or of L as open under it.
Section~\ref{sec:pwn-new} replays it in his Isabelle and Lean encodings.
Combining the world-invariant restriction with his two-conjunct restriction
gives a reading, Ax1GenTwoR, that meets his requirements (i) and (ii) and
still has genuine Nitpick countermodels to the actual existence of a
God-like being at every world and to collapse in K, and to Th3 on S4
frames.
His equivalence of Ax1GenTwo with Ax1Gen in the presence of nominals runs
through a world-dependent collection, which the rigid reading excludes, and a
genuine Nitpick countermodel confirms that Ax1GenTwoR does not give Ax1Gen
back.
The reading is small, and its Figure~8 and Scott forms are unchecked.
The Lean development of this repository is Lean~4.34.0.
Benzm\"uller's port of the AFP dataset \cite{benzmuellerLean2026} and the
package of \cite{benzmuellerPWN2026} are separate trails on Lean~4.33.1.
\end{abstract}

\section{The open problem, and its settlement}
\label{sec:open}

Three objects have to be kept apart, as Benzm\"uller and Scott set them out
\cite{benzmuellerScott2025}.
The first is G\"odel's own hand manuscript of 1970, reproduced as their
Figure~1.
The shelfmark they give is Kurt G\"odel Papers, Box~12, Folder~41, item
accession 060565.
The figure is printed with the copyright of the Institute for Advanced Study.
The second is the brief notes G\"odel showed Scott.
Those notes were very brief compared with the 1970 manuscript.
The third is the slightly modified version Scott discussed in a Princeton
seminar, printed as Appendix~B of Sobel
\cite{scott2004notes,sobel2004}.
The Archive of Formal Proofs entry of 7~January 2025, and the Lean
transcription in this repository, use the Scott-repaired essence of their
Figure~7 \cite{afpNotes2025}.
Neither file is a transcription of the hand page.
Nor is the 1970 essence manuscript the only ontological draft in the
Nachlass.
Lethen describes an early version, found in the Nachlass in 2018, that does
not rely on essential properties \cite{lethen2025}; this note goes on the
abstract of that paper and has not read its body.

In spring 1970, at the Princeton Philosophy Department, G\"odel asked Scott
confidentially to preserve some papers and notes in case of illness or death.
One of the notes was a brief sketch of an ontological argument.
Soon after, Scott mentioned the proof in a modal-logic seminar without
permission.
The version he mentioned was one he had slightly modified.
He records that he still feels embarrassed, and that he left Princeton soon
after for the chair in mathematical logic at Oxford and did not discuss the
matter with G\"odel \cite{benzmuellerScott2025}.

The hand manuscript's binary axiom says that the conjunction of two positive
properties is positive.
A footnote adds ``and for any number of summands'': the conjunction of a
collection of positive properties is itself positive.
Scott's seminar notes replace that generalization by a direct postulate that
being God-like is positive, his axiom A3
\cite{scott2004notes,benzmuellerScott2025}.

The hand definition of essence omits the exemplification conjunct
\(\varphi\,x\).
Benzm\"uller and Scott reconstruct the inconsistency from the empty-essence
lemma \((\lambda x.\,\bot)\,\mathrm{Ess.}\,x\).
The derivation of falsity they give is already valid in the modal logic K.
The Lean~4 port's \lean{\#print axioms} for \lean{Inconsistency} in
\lean{GoedelVariantHOML1}, on Lean~4.33.1, lists Ax2a, Ax3, Ax4, and
\lean{iNonempty}, the existence of at least one world, and no frame condition
\cite{benzmuellerLean2026}.
A cross-check in that paper replays the Isabelle proof after importing
\lean{HOMLinHOLonlyK}, under Isabelle2025-2.
The bound is on that proof.
The 1970 hand page remains Box~12, Folder~41, accession 060565, and the
port's Figure~7 module remains Scott's repaired essence.
Scott's addition of the conjunct \(\varphi\,x\) forces an essential property
of an individual to hold of that individual, blocks the empty-essence lemma,
and restores consistency of the package in their Figure~7
\cite{benzmuellerScott2025}.
The essence clause in Section~\ref{sec:fig7} is that repaired definition.
It is Scott's repair, not the hand manuscript's.

Jordan Howard Sobel later showed that Scott's premises collapse modality:
whatever is true is necessarily true \cite{sobel1987,sobel2004}.
The collapse has since been checked in Isabelle/HOL, going on the report of
Benzm\"uller and Fuenmayor, and so have repairs in the
spirit of Anderson and of Fitting
\cite{anderson1990,fitting2002,benzmuellerFuenmayor2020}.
Those repairs avoid collapse by weakening the axioms, and going on the
summary of M\"uhlenbeck and Benzm\"uller \cite{muehlenbeckBenzmueller2026}
the price is the ultrafilter maximality of the positive properties, so they
are not G\"odel's maximum unchanged.
Separately, the passage from ``possibly God-like'' to ``necessarily
God-like'' on the Scott side needs symmetry of accessibility, the modal axiom
B, and does not need the rest of S5 \cite{kanckos2017}.
Those facts are recorded again in Section~\ref{sec:related} so that they are
not mistaken for the theorems below.
For Figure~7, collapse is the question.
The S5 theory \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2} already proves
\[
  \mathrm{MC}:\qquad \valid{\varphi \supset \Nec\varphi},
\]
from Ax2a, Ax2b, Th5, the definition of God-likeness, and symmetry of \(R\)
\cite{afpNotes2025}.
Applied to the world-proposition ``the world is \(w\)'', that lemma already
confines \(R\) to the identity.
The same theory checks consistency only by \lean{nitpick[satisfy, card=1]}.

The question comes from a return to the footnote.
Christoph Benzm\"uller and Dana Scott, in the \emph{Monatshefte f\"ur
Mathematik} \cite{benzmuellerScott2025}, and in the matching Archive of Formal
Proofs entry \cite{afpNotes2025}, study the footnote's generalized conjunction
as Ax1Gen, the AFP abbreviation of that footnote.
Positivity of being God-like is not assumed.
It is lemma~L, derived from Ax1Gen.
The essence in that Figure~7 is Scott's, with the conjunct \(\varphi\,x\).
Individual quantifiers in the argument are actualist: an existence predicate
says who is around at a world.
In their Figure~7, Theorem Th3 is
\[
  \valid{\Pos(\exE x.\, G x) \supset \Nec(\exE y.\, G y)}.
\]
In the S5 embedding the proof uses symmetry of the accessibility relation and
nothing else from S5.
The theory \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2inS4} repeats the same non-logical axioms
over an S4 frame (reflexive and transitive, symmetry not assumed) and leaves
Th3 as \lean{oops}, with the comment ``Open problem''.
The header of that theory says that the proof of Th3 fails in S4.
Figure~8, which repairs the argument by changing necessary inclusion instead
of essence, has the same open \lean{oops} in
\lean{GoedelVariantHOML3inS4}.
The authors' formalization reports neither an S4 proof nor an S4 countermodel.
The \lean{oops} in \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2inS4} is that theory's Ax1Gen, the
literal abbreviation.
It is not a different reading of the axiom.
Their S5 proof of Th3 uses symmetry of accessibility, only the B schema; S5
is not required
\cite{benzmuellerScott2025,afpNotes2025}.
Figure~8 leaves the same \lean{oops}.
The Figure~8 goal is not the Isabelle check recorded here.

\paragraph{The questions are settled.}
Benzm\"uller's \emph{Proofs Without Nominals} \cite{benzmuellerPWN2026}
(arXiv v1 of 28~September 2026, v2 of 1~October 2026, read in full for this
revision, with its ancillary files) answers the three questions the
\emph{Monatshefte} notes leave open: Th3 of Figure~7 in S4; Th3 of Figure~8,
with the unique-essence lemmas; and T3 of Scott's variant with Ax1Gen in
place of A3.
Under Ax1Gen as the AFP states it, his Theorems~2 and~4 give Th3 of
Figure~7 in K, ``no countermodel exists in any normal modal logic'', and
collapse, by proofs that substitute only terms of the modal object language;
the proofs turn on the case in which \(\Phi\) is empty.
For Figure~8 he proves Th3 in K and the unique-essence lemmas hybrid-free in
K (his Theorem~9), and for Scott's variant T3 in K (Theorems~3 and~4).
He then emends the axiom.
Read to include the empty set and the singletons, the footnote's ``any number
of summands'' gives Ax1Gen, ``and the two offending instances are exactly
those degenerate ones''; his Ax1GenTwo requires at least two different
conjuncts at the world of evaluation \cite[Sect.~6]{benzmuellerPWN2026}.
Under Ax1GenTwo, Figure~7's Th3 holds hybrid-free and with no frame
condition when necessary existence is not the universal property or every
world has an actual individual, and unconditionally only with a nominal (his
Lemma~10, Theorem~11, Corollary~12).
In Figure~8, Th4 is no longer derivable (his Proposition~13); the results
return with Th4 postulated under a side condition (Theorem~15), or
hybrid-free in a variant with Figure~8's clause confined to the essence
(Theorem~16).
Scott's T3 holds under Ax1GenTwo (Theorem~14).
Once nominals are admitted, the two-conjunct restriction has no force: ``the
two-conjunct reading is a reading of the object language only''
\cite[Sect.~6.2]{benzmuellerPWN2026}.
His results are verified in Isabelle2025-2 and Lean~4.33.1, and his
hybrid-witness detectors classify every proof: ``A proof is hybrid-free if
every term it substitutes for a property or proposition variable is a term
of~\(\mathcal{L}\)'' \cite[Sect.~3]{benzmuellerPWN2026}.
What follows in this note was written before that paper.
Where it overlaps, his answers stand and this note's are superseded;
Section~\ref{sec:pwn-new} records what is left.

That check was run.
Session \lean{Ax1Gen\_EmptyAtHome\_Check} finished on Isabelle2025-2 against

The check is sound, but the instance is degenerate, and it is not evidence

  \qquad

Lemma \lean{fig7\_implies\_symmetric} is
\(\forall w\,v.\; w\,r\,v \longrightarrow v\,r\,w\), by that instance together

\section{Figure 7, as formalized}
\label{sec:fig7}

Worlds form a type \(W\), individuals a type \(\mathit{Ind}\), and
accessibility is a relation \(R\) on \(W\).
A world-proposition is a map \(W \to \mathsf{Prop}\).
A property is a map \(\mathit{Ind} \to W \to \mathsf{Prop}\), individual first
and world second, matching the AFP type \(e \Rightarrow i \Rightarrow
\mathsf{bool}\).
Positivity \(P\) is a world-relative predicate on properties.
Necessity and possibility are the usual quantifications along \(R\):
\[
  (\Nec\varphi)\,w \;\equiv\; \forall v.\; R\,w\,v \to \varphi\,v,
  \qquad
  (\Pos\varphi)\,w \;\equiv\; \exists v.\; R\,w\,v \land \varphi\,v.
\]
Validity \(\valid{\varphi}\) means \(\varphi\) holds at every world.

An existence predicate \(\mathit{ex} : \mathit{Ind} \to W \to \mathsf{Prop}\)
plays the role of the AFP constant \(\mathit{existsAt}\).
The outer type of individuals is fixed.
Who exists may vary.
Actualist quantifiers are the restrictions
\[
  (\allE y.\, \varphi\,y)\,w
    \;\equiv\; \forall y.\; \mathit{ex}\,y\,w \to \varphi\,y\,w,
  \qquad
  (\exE y.\, \varphi\,y)\,w
    \;\equiv\; \exists y.\; \mathit{ex}\,y\,w \land \varphi\,y\,w.
\]
Quantifiers over properties stay possibilist: they range over every map
\(\mathit{Ind} \to W \to \mathsf{Prop}\).

God-likeness, necessary inclusion, essence, and necessary existence are the
Figure~7 clauses.
Writing \(\varphi \mathbin{\cdot} \psi\) for the pointwise conjunction of
properties and \(\sim\varphi\) for pointwise negation:
\begin{align*}
  G\,x &\;\equiv\; \forall \varphi.\; P\,\varphi \supset \varphi\,x,\\
  \varphi \necImp \psi
    &\;\equiv\; \Nec(\allE y.\, \varphi\,y \supset \psi\,y),\\
  \varphi \mathbin{\mathrm{Ess.}} x
    &\;\equiv\; \varphi\,x \land
      (\forall \psi.\; \psi\,x \supset (\varphi \necImp \psi)),\\
  E\,x &\;\equiv\; \forall \varphi.\;
      (\varphi \mathbin{\mathrm{Ess.}} x) \supset \Nec(\exE z.\, \varphi\,z).
\end{align*}
The AFP binder in the last clause shadows \(x\); the Lean definition uses a
fresh variable, which is the same formula.
The axioms, all under validity, are Ax1 (binary conjunction), Ax2a (exclusive
disjunction of \(P\,\varphi\) and \(P(\sim\varphi)\)), Ax2b
(\(P\,\varphi \supset \Nec P\,\varphi\)), Ax3 (\(P\,E\)), and Ax4 (a positive
property passes positivity along \(\necImp\)).
Ax1 is part of the package and holds in the one-world model of
Section~\ref{sec:literal}.
Th3 does not use it.
The AFP proof of Th3 does not use it either.

The footnote becomes two abbreviations and one axiom.
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{PosProps}\,\Phi
    &\;\equiv\; \forall \varphi.\; \Phi\,\varphi \supset P\,\varphi,\\
  \mathrm{ConjOfPropsFrom}\,\varphi\,\Phi
    &\;\equiv\; \Nec\bigl(\allE z.\;
        \varphi\,z \leftrightarrow
        (\forall \psi.\; \Phi\,\psi \supset \psi\,z)\bigr),\\
  \mathrm{Ax1Gen}
    &\;\equiv\; \valid{(\mathrm{PosProps}\,\Phi \land
        \mathrm{ConjOfPropsFrom}\,\varphi\,\Phi) \supset P\,\varphi}.
\end{align*}
Both \(\Phi\) and \(\varphi\) are universally quantified.
This is the text of \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2} and of
\lean{GoedelVariantHOML2inS4}.
Lemma~L is \(\valid{P\,G}\), obtained by feeding Ax1Gen the collection of
properties that are positive at the world of evaluation.
It is a lemma, not an axiom.
Scott's later A3 postulates that same sentence outright
\cite{scott2004notes}.
The two packages are different, and Section~\ref{sec:related} is there to keep
them apart.

In the S5 theory, Th3 is proved from Th2 by the three steps the AFP script
writes down: existence of a God-like being implies necessary existence; the
diamond of the antecedent is therefore a diamond of a box; symmetry turns
that diamond-box back into a box.
The Lean theorem \lean{th3\_of\_symmetric} is that argument, with the witness
individual written out.
Reflexivity is not used.
Lemma \lean{MC} in the same theory is the collapse schema quoted in
Section~\ref{sec:open}.
Its script cites Ax2a, Ax2b, Th5, the definition of \(G\), and \(R_{\mathrm{symm}}\).

\section{What the encoding is}
\label{sec:encoding}

The development is a shallow embedding in Lean~4 \cite{demouraUllrich2021},
with no Mathlib library.
Hypotheses are propositions, passed as arguments.
There is no Lean \lean{axiom} declaration anywhere in the repository.
The host logic is the ordinary Lean kernel.
Where a proof uses classical reasoning, \lean{\#print axioms} says so
(Section~\ref{sec:kernel}).
Isabelle/HOL is classical \cite{nipkow2002}, so that use of classical logic
matches the host logic of the AFP entry.
It is still a different host.

The departures from the AFP text are these.

World-relative \(P\), actualist individual quantifiers, possibilist
quantifiers over properties, and the derivation of \(P(G)\) are the AFP
reading of their Figure~7.
The essence clause includes the conjunct \(\varphi\,x\).
Benzm\"uller and Scott attribute that conjunct to Scott's repair of the 1970
hand definition, which omits it \cite{benzmuellerScott2025}.
The Lean file uses the repaired clause.
These choices are not extra deviations from the AFP text.
Older modules in the same repository use a different argument order for
properties and a constant domain.
Those modules are not the encoding of this paper.

One consequence of Lean's \textsf{Prop} is worth saying before the proofs.
A proof of a negation may be a classical contradiction rather than a
construction of a counter-witness.
The symmetry argument is of that kind.
The countermodel of Section~\ref{sec:recon} is not: failure of Th3 at the source
is an explicit world, an explicit individual, and a computation.

That instantiation is the one Section~\ref{sec:open} records as finished.
Inside \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2inS4}, Ax1Gen applied to
\[
  \Phi \equiv (\lambda\psi\,u.\; u \neq w \land (\forall x.\; \neg\psi\,x\,u)),
  \qquad
  \varphi \equiv (\lambda x\,u.\; u = w)
\]
yields symmetry, and the AFP Th3 goal follows, with no extra
\(R_{\mathrm{symm}}\) axiom.
The host is Isabelle2025-2.
The trail is the finished session \lean{Ax1Gen\_EmptyAtHome\_Check}.
It is not the Lean axiom table of Section~\ref{sec:kernel}.
The collection is empty at \(w\), and \(\varphi\) is a nominal, so this is
a degenerate and hybrid instance in the sense of \cite{benzmuellerPWN2026}
(Section~\ref{sec:open}).

A further Lean trail for the AFP dataset itself is Benzm\"uller's port
\cite{benzmuellerLean2026}.
It is a lake package on Lean~4.33.1, 30 modules, one per Isabelle/HOL theory,
with no library beyond Lean~4's core.
Benzm\"uller reports that a comparison tool shipped with the port finds all
548 pairs of statements in agreement with the Isabelle/HOL sources.
This note has not repeated that comparison.
The proofs are explicit Lean terms.
Classical reasoning is enabled, because Lean's \textsf{Prop} is
intuitionistic, and the types of worlds and of individuals are postulated

\subsection{The collection}
\label{sec:collection}

Ax1Gen invites a definition of a property by the collection it conjoins.
The abbreviation does not require the collection to be the same collection at
every world.
\(\mathrm{PosProps}\,\Phi\) is evaluated at the world where the axiom is
applied.
\(\mathrm{ConjOfPropsFrom}\) puts \(\Phi\) under a box, so on the right-hand
side \(\Phi\) is read at successor worlds.

Fix a world \(w\) and define
\begin{align*}
  \varphi &\;:\equiv\; \lambda\,\_\,u.\; (u = w),\\
  \Phi &\;:\equiv\; \lambda\,\psi\,u.\;
    \bigl(u \neq w \land \forall x.\; \neg\, \psi\,x\,u\bigr).
\end{align*}
At \(w\), the clause \(u \neq w\) fails, so \(\Phi\) marks no property.
Positivity of ``every marked property'' is vacuous, and Ax1Gen still concludes
that \(\varphi\) is positive at \(w\).
At a successor \(u \neq w\), \(\Phi\) marks every property empty at \(u\).
The right-hand side of the biconditional asks such a property of an individual
and cannot hold, and the left-hand side, ``the world is \(w\)'', is false.
Both sides fail together.
Positivity, once granted at \(w\), is pushed along an edge by Ax2b.
At a far world \(v\) that cannot see \(w\), necessary inclusion of \(\varphi\)
in \(\sim\varphi\) is vacuous, because the antecedent never fires.
Ax4 passes positivity to the negation.
Ax2a refuses to let a property and its negation both be positive.

\begin{lemma}[\lean{pos\_of\_home}]
\label{lem:home}
From Ax1Gen alone: if \(\varphi\) holds of every individual that exists at
\(w\) whenever \(R\,w\,w\), then \(P\,\varphi\) holds at \(w\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Instantiate Ax1Gen at the collection \(\Phi\,\psi\,u :\equiv (u \neq w \land \psi = \varphi)\).
At \(w\) that collection marks nothing, so PosProps is vacuous.
At a successor equal to \(w\), the hypothesis supplies \(\varphi\) of every
existent, which is what the biconditional asks.
At a successor other than \(w\), the only marked property is \(\varphi\) itself,
so the biconditional is the identity.
Ax1Gen returns \(P\,\varphi\) at \(w\).
\end{proof}

The collection of Figure~\ref{fig:phi} is the case in which \(\varphi\) says
``the world is \(w\)''.
Lemma~\ref{lem:home} is the same trick for an arbitrary property.
It is what ties positivity to who happens to exist at \(w\).

\subsection{Reflexivity, the B schema, and the identity}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{refl\_of\_gen}]
\label{thm:refl}
From Ax1Gen and Ax2a, \(R\) is reflexive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
If \(\neg R\,w\,w\), the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:home} is vacuous for every
property.
Both \(\bot\) and \(\sim\bot\) are therefore positive at \(w\).
Ax2a forbids that pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{B\_schema}]
\label{thm:B}
From Ax1Gen, Ax2a, Ax2b, and Ax4, every world-proposition \(q\) satisfies
\(q \supset \Nec\Pos q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Suppose \(q\) holds at \(w\), and \(R\,w\,v\).
Let \(\varphi\) ignore the individual and hold at a world iff \(q\) does.
Lemma~\ref{lem:home} makes \(\varphi\) positive at \(w\): under reflexivity,
every existent at \(w\) has \(\varphi\), because \(q\) is true there.
Ax2b pushes positivity to \(v\).
If \(v\) saw no world at which \(q\) holds, then \(\varphi\) would necessarily
include \(\bot\) at \(v\).
Ax4 would make \(\bot\) positive, and a second use of Ax4 would make
\(\sim\bot\) positive.
Ax2a forbids that pair.
So \(v\) sees some world at which \(q\) holds.
\end{proof}

Comprehension is used: the property \(\varphi\) is the map
\(\lambda\,\_\,u.\; q\,u\), and ``the world is \(w\)'' is a world-proposition
of the same kind.
Theorem~\ref{thm:sym} is the case \(q \equiv (u = w)\).

\begin{theorem}[\lean{fig7\_implies\_symmetric}]
\label{thm:sym}
From Ax1Gen, Ax2a, Ax2b, and Ax4 it follows that \(R\) is symmetric.
Reflexivity and transitivity are not hypotheses of this implication.
Reflexivity is already a consequence of Ax1Gen and Ax2a
(Theorem~\ref{thm:refl}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Fix \(R\,w\,v\), and suppose \(\neg R\,v\,w\).
Use the collection of Section~\ref{sec:collection}.
Ax1Gen yields \(P\,\varphi\) at \(w\), and Ax2b pushes it to \(v\).
At \(v\), necessary inclusion of \(\varphi\) in \(\sim\varphi\) is vacuous,
because a successor of \(v\) cannot be \(w\).
Ax4 yields \(P(\sim\varphi)\) at \(v\), contradicting Ax2a.
Hence \(R\,v\,w\).
\end{proof}

Ax3 is not used for symmetry.
The same four hypotheses are unsatisfiable on the two-world chain of
Figure~\ref{fig:chain}.
That fact is \lean{fig7\_unsat\_on\_S4\_chain}.
The audit module restates it as \lean{literal\_unsat\_on\_R\_chain}.
That chain is reflexive, transitive, and not symmetric.
It is not a countermodel to literal Figure~7, because no positivity and no
existence predicate satisfy the axioms there.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{R\_is\_identity}]
\label{thm:id}
From Ax1Gen, Ax2a, Ax2b, Ax3, and Ax4,
\(R\,w\,v\) if and only if \(v = w\).
Symmetry is not a hypothesis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Theorem~\ref{thm:refl} gives the right-to-left direction.
For the other direction, let \(\varphi\) be ``the world is \(w\)''.
Lemma~\ref{lem:home} makes it positive at \(w\), and Ax2b carries that
positivity to any \(v\) with \(R\,w\,v\).
Theorem Th5, which is necessary existence of a God-like being and which the
AFP derives from Th3 and Th4, supplies at \(v\) an existing individual who is
God-like.
God-likeness means every positive property holds of that individual, so the
world \(v\) is \(w\).
The Lean proof of Th5 goes through \lean{th3\_fig7}, which uses symmetry.
The symmetry it uses is Theorem~\ref{thm:sym}, derived from the same axioms,
not an extra frame assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{MC}]
\label{thm:MC}
From the same five axioms, every world-proposition \(q\) satisfies
\(\valid{q \supset \Nec q}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
If \(q\) holds at \(w\) and \(R\,w\,v\), Theorem~\ref{thm:id} forces \(v = w\),
so \(q\) holds at \(v\).
\end{proof}

This is the schema of the AFP lemma \lean{MC}.
The AFP proof assumes \(R_{\mathrm{symm}}\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:MC} does not.
The discrete-frame consequence is the same one their lemma already had, once
symmetry was known: applied to ``the world is \(w\)'', collapse leaves only
the reflexive loop.
What the literal S4 problem was missing is that symmetry does not have to be
assumed.
The axioms produce it, and they produce the identity relation.

From Ax1Gen, Ax2a, Ax2b, Ax3, and Ax4,

\subsection{The literal axiom is not the footnote}
\label{sec:gloss}

Theorem~\ref{thm:th3} is a theorem of the literal AFP abbreviation Ax1Gen.
The 1970 hand manuscript states binary Ax1 and a footnote.
It does not state that abbreviation.

The footnote says that a conjunction of positive properties is positive
\cite{goedel1995,benzmueller2026}.
The gloss printed with the proof says that positive means positive in the
moral aesthetic sense, independently of the accidental structure of the world
\cite{goedel1995}.
The AFP abbreviation does something else.
Its type lets \(\Phi\) vary by world.
Lemma~\ref{lem:home} then says that Ax1Gen alone makes every property true of
all existents at \(w\) positive at \(w\), whenever \(w\) sees itself, and makes
every property positive at \(w\) if \(w\) does not see itself.
Positivity at \(w\) tracks who exists at \(w\).
That is the accidental structure the gloss excludes.

The split is what a shallow embedding does if PosProps is left outside a box
while \(\Phi\) still depends on the world.
It is the text of \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2}, faithfully transcribed.
It is not a misprint, and it is not a reading of the footnote.
The footnote does not mention worlds.
The hand page is not the AFP text.
Theorem~\ref{thm:th3} is a theorem of the abbreviation, read so that the two
halves of Ax1Gen consult \(\Phi\) at different worlds.
It is also a theorem of the empty instance.
Under Ax1GenTwo the instance used by Lemma~\ref{lem:home} is unavailable,
because its collection marks nothing at \(w\); what Ax1GenTwo still yields there is the
subject of \cite[Sect.~6]{benzmuellerPWN2026}, not of this note.
Since the hand manuscript states only binary Ax1 and the footnote, and its
essence lacks the conjunct \(\varphi\,x\), neither Ax1Gen nor Ax1GenTwo is a
transcription of it; both are readings of the footnote placed into Scott's
repaired Figure~7.

\section{Two reconstructions}
\label{sec:recon}

Everything in Figure~7 stays put except Ax1Gen.
There are two replacements.
Neither is dictated by the footnote.
Both are reconstructions.
What both remove is \(\Phi\) being read at two different worlds by the two
halves of the axiom.
One may prefer the second because ``any number of summands'' is a single
collection, and a single collection does not change with the world.
The footnote still does not say so.
Rigidity, in the sense of Ax2b, is kept in all three readings.
The Lean name \lean{Ax1GenRigid} means that \(\Phi\) is world-invariant.
It does not mean that positivity is rigid.

\paragraph{R1, positivity in the box.}
Both halves are judged at the same accessible worlds:
\[
  \valid{\Nec\bigl(\mathrm{PosProps}\,\Phi \land
    \allE z.\; \varphi\,z \leftrightarrow
    (\forall \psi.\; \Phi\,\psi \supset \psi\,z)\bigr)
    \supset P\,\varphi}.
\]
Call this \lean{Ax1GenInBox}.
In~K, a box of a conjunction is a conjunction of boxes, and
ConjOfPropsFrom is already a box, so the displayed form is equivalent to
\[
  \valid{(\Nec(\mathrm{PosProps}\,\Phi) \land
    \mathrm{ConjOfPropsFrom}\,\varphi\,\Phi) \supset P\,\varphi},
\]
which is \lean{Ax1GenBox}.
The equivalence \lean{ax1GenBox\_iff\_inBox} depends on no kernel axioms.
One countermodel covers both spellings.
Benzm\"uller's Ax1GenBox \cite[Sect.~6.3]{benzmuellerPWN2026} adds
\(\Nec\mathrm{PosProps}\,\Phi\) to the literal antecedent; on reflexive frames
it is this reading.
He finds two-world countermodels to the actual existence of a God-like
being at every world and to collapse in K, and to Th3 in S4, and shows that it fails his requirement~(ii), Th4 from the
conjunction axiom and Ax2a alone.
The R1 countermodel of Theorem~\ref{thm:r12} is therefore a rediscovery.

\paragraph{R2, a world-invariant collection.}
Restrict Ax1Gen to collections that do not depend on the world:
\(\Phi\,\psi\,w \leftrightarrow \Phi\,\psi\,v\) for every property and every pair of
worlds.
PosProps and ConjOfPropsFrom stay literal.
Call this \lean{Ax1GenRigid}.
R2 moves no box.
It freezes \(\Phi\).
The collection of Figure~\ref{fig:phi} is not world-invariant, so R2 does not
apply to it.

\subsection{Lemma L still derives}

Under R1, lemma~L is immediate from the repaired axiom alone
(\lean{lemma\_L\_box}, \lean{lemma\_L\_inBox}).
Take \(\Phi\) to be \(P\) itself.
At every world, every property that \(\Phi\) marks is positive there, by
identity, so the extra box is free.
The conjunction of whatever is positive at a world is \(G\) at that world, by
the definition of \(G\).

Under R2 the same \(\Phi\) may be refused, because \(P\) itself need not be
world-invariant.
Take a snapshot at the world \(w\) where the lemma is being proved:
\(\Phi\,\psi\,\_ :\equiv P\,\psi\,w\).
That collection ignores its world argument.
At a successor \(v\), the conjunction it defines is \(G\) only if positivity at
\(w\) and positivity at \(v\) agree.
Ax2a and Ax2b give that agreement along an edge
(\lean{pos\_agree}): a positive property stays positive by Ax2b, and a
property positive at \(v\) cannot have a positive negation at \(w\), or Ax2b
would carry the negation forward and break Ax2a at \(v\).
So \lean{lemma\_L\_rigid} needs Ax2a and Ax2b.
Literal \lean{lemma\_L} needed neither.
Benzm\"uller's ancillary session~B states the same restriction, Ax1GenR, and
records ``L: derivability open'', since the derivation with \(\Phi := P\) is
unavailable when \(P\) is not rigid; its Lean module leaves the lemma as an
\lean{openproblem}, that is, a \lean{sorry} \cite{benzmuellerPWN2026}.
Our \lean{lemma\_L\_rigid} builds on Benzm\"uller's AFP entry \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2inS4}~\cite{afpNotes2025}, and its snapshot-and-agreement step uses the same move as \lean{Th4\_finite} in arXiv:2608.07578~\cite{benzmueller2026}; our commit \lean{b23cc28} cited arXiv:2608.07578, for a different result, 31~minutes before \lean{27524f7}, and our records do not show whether \lean{Th4\_finite} was read.
It proves L for the rigid-\(\Phi\) variant he later lists as Candidate~B (Ax1GenR), in commit \lean{27524f7}, merged 24~September 2026 (20:51~UTC), four days before arXiv:2609.36279 v1 (28~September)~\cite{benzmuellerPWN2026}.
We checked arXiv:2609.26806 v1~\cite{benzmuellerLean2026}, the Monatshefte paper~\cite{benzmuellerScott2025}, the AFP entries, and his public repositories as of that date and found no statement of Candidate~B or of L as open under it.
Section~\ref{sec:pwn-new} replays it.
The box in R1 re-reads \(\Phi := P\) at the successor rather than freezing it
at the source.

Symmetry is not a consequence of either reconstruction.
The proof is the chain in Figure~\ref{fig:chain} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:r12}: the frame is not symmetric, and each reconstruction
holds on it.
The collection in Theorem~\ref{thm:sym} does not witness a contradiction under
either reconstruction.
It is empty at the source, so source PosProps holds, but at a successor it
marks every property empty at that world, and in particular it marks \(\bot\).
R1's boxed PosProps demands \(P(\bot)\) there.
R2 does not apply, because the collection is not world-invariant.
That is why the derivation of a back-edge has nothing left to instantiate.
It is not itself the proof that symmetry can fail.

Let the worlds be the two Booleans.
The source \(\mathsf{false}\) sees both worlds.
The sink \(\mathsf{true}\) sees only itself

The relation is reflexive and transitive.
It is not symmetric: the sink does not see the source
(\lean{R\_chain\_not\_symmetric}).
The domain has one element, and that individual exists at both worlds.
Existence is the constant true predicate.
The actualist restriction never excludes anyone, so this countermodel does
not exercise the actualist machinery.
Positivity ignores the world at which it is asked and reads the sink:
\[
  P\,\varphi \;\equiv\; \varphi\,()\,\mathsf{true}.
\]
A property is positive precisely when the individual has it at \(t\).

At the sink, God-likeness is a tautology.
\(G\) says that every positive property holds of the individual there, and
positivity means that the property holds of the individual there
(\lean{chain\_god\_sink}; no kernel axioms).
At the source it fails.
The property ``the world is the sink'' holds at the sink, hence is positive,
and the individual does not have it at the source
(\lean{chain\_not\_god\_source}; no kernel axioms).

\begin{theorem}[\lean{chain\_th3\_fails\_at\_source}]
\label{thm:fails}
At the source, \(\Pos(\exE G)\) holds and \(\Nec(\exE G)\) fails.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The source sees the sink, where the individual exists and is God-like.
The source also sees itself, where that same individual exists and is not
God-like.
The diamond is the first fact.
The failure of the box is the second.
\end{proof}

No kernel axiom is used.
The packaged denial \lean{chain\_not\_Th3} is the same fact pulled back along
the definition of Th3.
\lean{chain\_not\_necessary\_existence} is the failure of the box as a
validity, equally axiom-free.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{r1\_s4\_countermodel}, \lean{r2\_s4\_countermodel}]
\label{thm:r12}
On this frame the relation is an S4 frame and not a symmetric one.
Ax1, Ax2a, Ax2b, Ax3, Ax4, and both spellings of R1 hold, and Th3 fails.
The same frame, the same positivity, and the same existence predicate satisfy
R2, and Th3 fails.
\end{theorem}

The axiom checks are direct, and most of them use no kernel axioms.
Ax2a is the exclusive-or of ``\(\varphi\) holds at the sink'' and ``\(\varphi\)
fails at the sink'', which is classical excluded middle
(\lean{chain\_ax2a}).
Ax2b is immediate because \(P\) does not look at its world argument: positivity
is already global.
Ax4 follows because both worlds see the sink, so a necessary inclusion can be
read there, where \(P\) evaluates extensions.
Ax3 holds because \(P\) on necessary existence only asks for the extension at
the sink, and the essence hypothesis at the sink includes that the property
holds of the individual there.
For R1, the boxed biconditional at whichever world one starts from can be read
at the sink, which both worlds see, and the right-hand side at the sink is
exactly the extension \(P\) consults.
For R2 the same computation works, and world-invariance lets a mark at the
sink be copied back to the world where PosProps was assumed.

\begin{proposition}[\lean{literal\_to\_R1}, \lean{literal\_to\_R2},
  \lean{readings\_coincide\_on\_unit}]
\label{prop:weaker}
Literal Ax1Gen implies R1 on every reflexive frame, and implies R2 on every
frame.
On a one-world identity frame the three readings coincide.
On the chain, this positivity satisfies both reconstructions and does not
satisfy literal Ax1Gen.
\end{proposition}

One direction is \lean{literal\_to\_R1}.
The other is \lean{literal\_to\_R2}.
The one-world coincidence is \lean{readings\_coincide\_on\_unit}.
Every collection on a one-world frame is world-invariant, and along the
identity relation a box does not leave the world.
If this positivity satisfied literal Ax1Gen, Theorem~\ref{thm:sym} would make
the chain symmetric.
\lean{chain\_not\_literal\_Ax1Gen} records that it does not.
The converse implications, reconstruction implies literal, are not proved in
general.
They are proved on the one-world frame, and the chain separates the readings
in the other direction: the reconstructions have a model there and the literal
axiom has none.

\label{sec:pwn-new}

against the ancillary files of arXiv:2609.36279v2.
The theories are in \lean{pwn36279/} of this repository; they import his

In his notation, \(P\) is positivity, \(G\) God-likeness, and variant~2 is

\subsection{Lemma L for a world-invariant collection}

His session~B restricts Ax1Gen to collections that do not depend on the
world, as R2 does:
\[
  \mathrm{Ax1GenR}:\quad
  \forall \Phi{::}(e \Rightarrow \sigma) \Rightarrow \mathit{bool}.\;
  \valid{\bigl(\mathrm{PosPropsR}\,\Phi \land \mathrm{ConjR}\,\varphi\,\Phi\bigr)
    \supset P\,\varphi},
\]
with \(\mathrm{PosPropsR}\,\Phi\,w \equiv \forall\psi.\,\Phi\,\psi
\longrightarrow P\,\psi\,w\) and \(\mathrm{ConjR}\,\varphi\,\Phi \equiv
\Nec(\allE z.\, \varphi\,z \leftrightarrow
(\lambda w.\,\forall\psi.\,\Phi\,\psi \longrightarrow \psi\,z\,w))\).
There a Nitpick search finds no countermodel to L for at most two worlds and
one individual, and the session's README (\lean{alternatives/README.md},
line~87) records ``derivability \textbf{open} (the article's derivation,
\(\Phi := P\), is unavailable since \(P\) is not rigid)''; line~150 of his
\lean{Ax1GenRigidVariant2.lean} is
\lean{example (\_ : Ax1GenR) : \(\lfloor\)P G\(\rfloor\) := by openproblem}.
This is his Candidate~B, not his Candidate~A.
Candidate~A, Ax1GenBox, keeps a world-dependent \(\Phi\) and adds
\(\Nec\mathrm{PosProps}\,\Phi\) to \(\mathrm{PosProps}\,\Phi\); under it L
does hold, and the Lean theorem \lean{Th4\_rigid} of his
\lean{Ax1GenBoxVariant2.lean} is named for its rigid witness sets \(\{G\}\)
and \(\emptyset\), not for a rigid reading of the axiom.
Our \lean{lemma\_L\_rigid} concerns his Candidate~B, Ax1GenR, with rigid
\(\Phi\), \(\mathrm{PosPropsR}\,\Phi \equiv \lambda w.\,\forall\psi.\,
\Phi\,\psi \longrightarrow P\,\psi\,w\), and \(\mathrm{ConjR}\), not
Ax1GenBox.
Our \lean{lemma\_L\_rigid} builds on Benzm\"uller's AFP entry \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2inS4}~\cite{afpNotes2025}, and its snapshot-and-agreement step uses the same move as \lean{Th4\_finite} in arXiv:2608.07578~\cite{benzmueller2026}; our commit \lean{b23cc28} cited arXiv:2608.07578, for a different result, 31~minutes before \lean{27524f7}, and our records do not show whether \lean{Th4\_finite} was read.
It proves L for the rigid-\(\Phi\) variant he later lists as Candidate~B (Ax1GenR), in commit \lean{27524f7}, merged 24~September 2026 (20:51~UTC), four days before arXiv:2609.36279 v1 (28~September)~\cite{benzmuellerPWN2026}.
We checked arXiv:2609.26806 v1~\cite{benzmuellerLean2026}, the Monatshefte paper~\cite{benzmuellerScott2025}, the AFP entries, and his public repositories as of that date and found no statement of Candidate~B or of L as open under it.

\begin{proposition}[\lean{L\_R\_derived}]
\label{prop:LR}
In variant~2, Ax1GenR, Ax2a, and Ax2b yield \(\valid{P\,G}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
At \(w\), take the snapshot \(\Phi\,\psi :\equiv P\,\psi\,w\).
PosPropsR holds at \(w\) by definition.
Along an edge \(w\,R\,v\), Ax2b carries positivity forward, and Ax2a with
Ax2b carries it back (\lean{pos\_agree}), so the conjunction of the snapshot
at \(v\) is \(G\) at \(v\).
Ax1GenR returns \(P\,G\) at \(w\).
\end{proof}

This is \lean{lemma\_L\_rigid} of Section~\ref{sec:recon}, restated in his
encoding.
Trail: Isabelle2025-2 session \lean{PwnCheckRigid}, importing his session
\lean{Ax1GenAlternativesB}; and in Lean~4.33.1 a copy of his module
\lean{Ax1GenRigidVariant2} with the \lean{openproblem} replaced, where
\lean{\#print axioms L\_R\_derived} lists Ax2a, Ax2b, the signature
constants \lean{P}, \lean{R}, \lean{e}, \lean{existsAt}, \lean{i}, and
\lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice}, \lean{Quot.sound}, with no
\lean{sorry}.
His \lean{\#hybrid\_audit} does not flag the proof.
That is weaker than it sounds: the snapshot has type
\((e \to \sigma) \to \mathsf{Prop}\), which is not a property or proposition
variable and so lies outside the detector's definition, and a snapshot of
positivity at a named world behaves like a satisfaction operator.
Whether this proof is hybrid-free is not settled here.
One consequence does not depend on that question.
His session~B refutes the actual existence of a God-like being at every
world, collapse in K, and Th3 in S4 under Ax1GenR with L as an added
hypothesis.
Since Ax1GenR already yields L, those are countermodels of Ax1GenR alone;
\lean{PwnCheckRigidNitpick} re-runs two of them without the added hypothesis
and Nitpick reports them genuine.

\subsection{A rigid two-conjunct reading}

Combining the world-invariant restriction with his two-conjunct restriction
gives
\[
  \mathrm{Ax1GenTwoR}:\quad
  \forall \Phi{::}(e \Rightarrow \sigma) \Rightarrow \mathit{bool}.\;
  \valid{\bigl((\exists \psi_1\,\psi_2.\; \Phi\,\psi_1 \land \Phi\,\psi_2
     \land \psi_1 \neq \psi_2) \land \mathrm{PosPropsR}\,\Phi \land
     \mathrm{ConjR}\,\varphi\,\Phi\bigr) \supset P\,\varphi}.
\]
It is implied by Ax1GenR and by Ax1GenTwo (\lean{TwoR\_weaker\_than\_R},
\lean{TwoR\_weaker\_than\_Two}).
With \(\Phi = \{\psi_1, \psi_2\}\) it gives binary Ax1 for distinct
conjuncts.

\begin{proposition}[checked in Isabelle2025-2]
\label{prop:TwoR}
In variant~2:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Ax1GenTwoR with Ax2a, Ax2b, Ax3, and Ax4 yields \(\valid{P\,G}\)
  (\lean{L\_TwoR}, session \lean{PwnCheckRigid}); this is his
  requirement~(i).
\item In his session~\lean{Th4FromConjunction}, whose only axiom is Ax2a,
  Nitpick finds genuine countermodels to Th4 and to \(P(\lambda x.\top)\)
  from Ax1GenTwoR (\lean{PwnCheckReqII}), and to Th4 from Ax1GenTwoR with
  Ax2b added (\lean{PwnCheckReqIIbB}); this is his requirement~(ii).
\item With all axioms of variant~2, Nitpick finds genuine countermodels to
  \(\valid{\exE x.\,G\,x}\) and to collapse \(\valid{\varphi \supset
  \Nec\varphi}\) in K, and to Th3 on reflexive transitive frames
  (\lean{PwnCheckRigidNitpick}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The countermodels are certified by \lean{expect = genuine} annotations, which
make the build fail otherwise; a control session (\lean{PwnSanity}), in which
a valid formula carries the same annotation, fails with ``Unexpected outcome:
none'', which confirms that the annotation is enforced.
A countermodel certified genuine refutes its statement whatever the size of
the model, so the small cardinalities searched (at most two worlds, and one
or two individuals) limit only the searches that came back ``none'' or
``unknown''.
Requirement~(ii) in particular is certified, not merely unrefuted: in
\lean{PwnCheckReqII}, inside his session \lean{Th4FromConjunction} whose only
axiom is Ax2a, \lean{Th4\_TwoR\_alone} and \lean{PTop\_TwoR\_alone} carry
\lean{expect = genuine}, and so does the version with Ax2b added in
\lean{PwnCheckReqIIbB}.

Two things make this worth recording.
First, his assessment \cite[Sect.~6.4]{benzmuellerPWN2026} says of his four
readings that ``the world-dependence of \(\Phi\) is what produces the
collapse, and only Ax1GenBox removes that. The price is (ii).''
Among those four readings that is right.
Ax1GenTwoR removes the world-dependence and keeps~(ii).
Second, his equivalence of Ax1GenTwo with Ax1Gen once nominals are admitted
(\cite[Sect.~6.2, Theorem~16(d)]{benzmuellerPWN2026}, Isabelle
\lean{Two\_equivalent} in his session~E, which he says goes through
unchanged in variant~2) does not transfer.
In his session~E that equivalence is \lean{Ax1Gen\_of\_Two}, which uses
\lean{R\_Id}, which uses collapse at the nominal \(\lambda u.\, u = w\) and
so the existence of a God-like being at every world; that in turn comes
from his Theorem~11, whose collection \(\Phi\) depends on whether a God-like
being exists at the world where it is read.
A rigid \(\Phi\) cannot do that, and this is the reason the argument does
not apply to Ax1GenTwoR.
The non-equivalence itself is checked separately: with all axioms of
variant~2, Nitpick finds a genuine countermodel to ``Ax1GenTwoR implies
Ax1Gen'' (\lean{Ax1Gen\_of\_TwoR} in \lean{PwnCheckRigidNitpick}).

The limits are as plain.
His requirement~(iii) is that an emendation \emph{need not} block collapse,
which comes with the maximality of the positive properties; blocking it is
not a merit by his criteria, and it is not claimed as one here.
Ax2a, kept in both directions, is the maximality half of an ultrafilter, and
binary Ax1 holds; whether the models of Proposition~\ref{prop:TwoR}(c) keep
ultrafilter maximality in the sense of M\"uhlenbeck and Benzm\"uller is going
on their summary \cite{muehlenbeckBenzmueller2026}, not checked.
Collapse in KB or S5 under Ax1GenTwoR was not checked.
The Notes' S5 derivation of collapse uses the conjunction axiom only through
L, so it is expected to return there, as it does for Ax1GenBox.
The Figure~8 and Scott forms of Ax1GenTwoR were not checked.
Whether a quantifier over world-invariant sets of properties belongs to the
modal object language is itself a question; Ax1GenR and Ax1GenTwoR are
readings stated in the embedding.
The proofs are elementary.

\section{What was already known}
\label{sec:related}

Several nearby statements are in the same repository, and they were already
theorems of other people.
They are named here so that the results above are not asked to carry them.

Sobel showed that Scott-style premises yield \(\varphi \supset \Nec\varphi\)
\cite{sobel1987,sobel2004}.
Benzm\"uller and Fuenmayor encoded Scott's, Anderson's, and Fitting's
variants and checked that Scott's intensional positivity collapses modality,
while Anderson's emendation and Fitting's extensional positivity do not
\cite{benzmuellerFuenmayor2020,anderson1990,andersonGettings1996,fitting2002}.
The repository's collapse modules rediscover the Scott-side collapse, including
the world-relative variant in which collapse along \(R\) is separated from
Sobel's global biconditional when \(R\) is not universal.
That rediscovery is not Theorem~\ref{thm:MC}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:MC} is collapse for the literal Figure~7 package, and the
prior art for that schema is the AFP lemma \lean{MC}, which assumes symmetry
\cite{afpNotes2025}.

Kanckos and Woltzenlogel Paleo gave a proof of Scott's argument in KB
\cite{kanckos2017}.
Benzm\"uller and Scott make the same point for their Th3.
In the proof they construct for the repaired-essence Figure~7, only symmetry
of accessibility is used: only the B schema; S5 is not required
\cite{benzmuellerScott2025,afpNotes2025}.
The published theories report no S4 proof and no S4 countermodel, and
Figure~8 leaves the same \lean{oops}.
The Figure~7 \lean{oops} is the literal abbreviation in
\lean{GoedelVariantHOML2inS4}.
The probe of Section~\ref{sec:open} proves that goal by deriving symmetry.
Theorem~\ref{thm:th3} is the Lean theorem of the same abbreviation, and it
also forces the identity.
Benzm\"uller reports that the port leaves Th3 open, as an anonymous sorry,
in the repaired S4 restrictions \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2inS4},
\lean{GoedelVariantHOML2possInS4}, \lean{GoedelVariantHOML3inS4}, and
\lean{GoedelVariantHOML3possInS4} \cite{benzmuellerLean2026}.
The port proves Th3 of \lean{GoedelVariantHOML1inS4} from Ax3 and \lean{Rrefl}.

Its Table~4, from \lean{\#print axioms} on Lean~4.33.1, reports that the S5
proofs of Th5 and \lean{MC} in \lean{GoedelVariantHOML2} consume an assumed
\(R_{\mathrm{symm}}\), and that Scott's T3 and \lean{MC} consume only
symmetry.
That is an upper bound on those proofs.
It is the prior-art pattern, symmetry assumed, and it is not the derivation
of symmetry from Ax1Gen, Ax2a, Ax2b, and Ax4.
The reconstructions do not: Theorem~\ref{thm:r12} fails on the chain.
Figure~8's \lean{oops} is not discharged by the probe.
The repository's Scott-style development rediscovers the positive half
(local Th3 from symmetry alone, on that encoding) and pairs it with
countermodels once symmetry is dropped.
Those countermodels postulate positivity of being God-like.
Figure~7 derives that sentence as lemma~L.
A countermodel that assumes the lemma does not answer a question whose point
is what happens when the lemma is earned from Ax1Gen.
The file \lean{S4Open.lean} in the repository is an explicit record of this
mismatch.
Theorem~\ref{thm:r12} is not that countermodel.
On the chain of Figure~\ref{fig:chain} the literal axiom has no model at all.

Anderson's emendation---only one direction of the exclusion axiom, and
God-likeness as possession of exactly the positive properties, necessarily---is
a known repair \cite{anderson1990,andersonGettings1996}.
A thin fragment of it is formalized elsewhere in the repository, and a
two-world witness there keeps a contingent fact alive.
That fragment is a rediscovery of Anderson's repair.
Fitting's extensional block is not formalized \cite{fitting2002}.
Neither is part of Theorems~\ref{thm:id} and~\ref{thm:r12}.

The early mechanizations of the Scott-style argument
\cite{benzmuellerPaleo2014} are the same Scott package.
Benzm\"uller's note on ultrafilters \cite{benzmueller2026} derives
\(\valid{\Nec(\exE x.\, G x)}\) already in K, from an ultrafilter axiom U1, a
conjunction principle, an entailment axiom, and the definition of \(G\).
The note is explicit that this is a simplified package, and that Scott's own

It is not Figure~7's axiom list, and it does not use the AFP scoping of
Ax1Gen.
Two further statements in that comment are citations, not theorems of this

Binary Ax1 admits an infinite single-world countermodel in which positivity

The \emph{Monatshefte} paper already records the matching positive fact: Th4

The Archive entry on simplified variants \cite{afpSimplified2021} studies
arguments that avoid essence and necessary existence and that are valid in K

\section{Conclusion}

The three questions this note set out from are settled, by
\cite{benzmuellerPWN2026}, under Ax1Gen in K by hybrid-free proofs, and
again under his two-conjunct emendation Ax1GenTwo, with side conditions and,
for Figure~8, a further emendation.
This note's own answer, Th3 in S4 through derived symmetry and the identity
of \(R\), is superseded.
It was obtained from an instance of Ax1Gen that is empty at the world of
evaluation and names that world, so it is degenerate in his sense and its
witnesses are hybrid.
It is a theorem of the shallow embedding of the AFP text, not of the modal
object language, and not evidence about G\"odel's footnote.
The essence used throughout is Scott's repaired clause, with the conjunct
\(\varphi\,x\), as in the AFP Figure~7; it is not the essence definition of
the 1970 hand manuscript \cite{benzmuellerScott2025}, and that manuscript
is not the only ontological draft in the Nachlass \cite{lethen2025}.

What remains is small.
For the world-invariant reading of the footnote, which Benzm\"uller's session~B
leaves open, lemma~L follows from Ax2a and Ax2b by a snapshot of
positivity (Proposition~\ref{prop:LR}), so his countermodels with L added
are countermodels of that reading alone.
Combining that reading with his two-conjunct restriction gives Ax1GenTwoR,
which meets his requirements (i) and (ii) and, in variant~2, has genuine
countermodels to the existence of a God-like being at every world and to
collapse in K, and to Th3 in S4 (Proposition~\ref{prop:TwoR}).
Unlike Ax1GenTwo, it does not collapse back to Ax1Gen when nominals are
admitted: his route to that equivalence needs a world-dependent collection,
and Nitpick certifies a model of Ax1GenTwoR in which Ax1Gen fails.
Whether the snapshot is hybrid-free, whether Ax1GenR yields Th4 once Ax2b is
present, and how Ax1GenTwoR behaves in KB, in Figure~8, and in Scott's
variant, are not settled here.
The comparison of Section~\ref{sec:recon} stands as a comparison: where
Ax1Gen reads \(\Phi\) matters, and \cite[Sect.~6.4]{benzmuellerPWN2026}
reaches the same diagnosis, that the world-dependence of \(\Phi\) produces
the collapse.
